Poetry Toolbox 🧰

Use this quick guide while you work.

LineOne row of words in a poem.
StanzaA group of lines, similar to a paragraph.
RhymeWords with similar ending sounds.
RepetitionRepeating words or sounds for emphasis.
AlliterationRepeating beginning consonant sounds.
OnomatopoeiaA word that imitates a sound.
SimileA comparison using like or as.
MetaphorA comparison saying one thing is another.
PersonificationHuman qualities given to nonhuman things.
ToneThe speaker's or author's attitude.
MoodThe feeling the reader gets.
ThemeA message or lesson developed through the text.
FAST Reader Rule: FIND IT → THINK ABOUT IT → CONNECT IT.
DEVICE → EFFECT → MEANING

Read the New Poem 🎒

First read: What is happening? Second read: Notice structure, sounds, figurative language, mood changes, and the message.

Between the Bells

My locker coughs and clicks at eight, The hallway hums, a rushing stream. Backpacks bump and sneakers skate, I chase the tail of one more dream.
At lunch the room becomes a drum, Voices bounce from wall to wall. A friend's quick joke makes worry numb, And suddenly I stand up tall.
By afternoon, the clock crawls slow, A quiz sits waiting on my screen. I breathe in deep, then let it go, And find I'm braver than I seem.
The final bell rings bright and clear, We spill outside into the sun. Some days feel heavy while they're here, Then somehow, still, we get them done.
Reader Mission: This poem is about an ordinary school day, but the poet uses structure and poetic devices to show how pressure can change into confidence and relief.

Mission 1: Structure & Rhyme 🧱

Teach It: Lines are grouped into stanzas. Poets use those groups to organize ideas, moments, and shifts.

Model It: Look at Stanza 1. It introduces the rushed beginning of the school day. Stanza 4 moves to the end of the day.
Strong-reader thinking: The stanzas help move the reader through different parts of the day, so the structure supports the poem's progression.

Mission 2: Figurative Language 🎨

Teach It: Figurative language creates pictures and feelings. A strong reader explains the effect, not just the device.

Model It: “At lunch the room becomes a drum.”
Think: This metaphor compares the lunchroom to a drum. It helps the reader imagine a loud, booming, energetic space.

Mission 6: Context & Paraphrasing 🔤

Teach It: Context clues help unlock word meaning. Paraphrasing means restating the author's idea accurately in your own words.

Model It: “A friend's quick joke makes worry numb.”
Think: The joke helps reduce the speaker's worry, so numb means the worry becomes less noticeable or less intense.
